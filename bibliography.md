<details>
  <summary><b> Blanchflower, David G. “Is Happiness U-Shaped Everywhere? Age and Subjective Well-Being in 145 Countries.” Journal of Population Economics, vol. 34, no. 2, 2021, pp. 575–624. PubMed Central, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7480662/. Accessed 19 July 2026
 </b></summary>

In this journal article, Blanchflower examines the relationship between age and subjective well-being across 145 developed and developing countries. This article uses information from fourteen different survey series, and the author argues that happiness generally follows a U-shaped pattern: it declines from young adulthood, reaches its lowest point around middle age, and then begins to rise again later in life. According to Blanchflower, the lowest point in happiness occurs during “midlife around age 50” (575). This can be seen in both developing and advanced countries, leading him to conclude that “the happiness curve seems to be everywhere” (575). The author also controls for factors such as education, employment, and marital status in order to separate the effects of age from other personal factors. The study draws from a large and geographically diverse sample rather than focusing only on wealthy Western countries. Still, because much of the evidence comes from surveys comparing different people at one point in time, the study cannot fully separate the effects of aging from generational and cultural differences. This means that some of the changes in happiness may be shaped not only by age, but also by the different social conditions experienced by each generation.
